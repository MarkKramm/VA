title: Clean and Organise a Small Client Dataset
summary: >-
  Take a deliberately messy client contact list, make it reliable, and hand it
  back with a change log that makes every transformation reversible.
purpose: >-
  Proves you can improve data somebody else depends on without deleting anything,
  guessing at anything, or leaving a change you cannot account for.
difficulty: beginner
estimatedMinutes: 75
skills:
  - data-entry
  - data-cleaning
  - data-quality-control
prerequisites:
  - id: cleaning-a-messy-sheet
    reason: The method this task is judged against — standardise, mark, log — is set out there.
  - id: spreadsheet-basics
    reason: You will be editing a sheet with formulas in it, and the editing rules apply throughout.
scenario: >-
  A client sends you a contact list exported from a system they have used for
  three years. It has around sixty rows. Duplicate companies appear under slightly
  different names, dates are in two formats, some phone numbers have spaces and
  some do not, and three rows are missing an email address entirely. There is a
  formula column counting contacts per company, and a second sheet the client uses
  for a mail merge. The client asks you to "make it usable".
requirements:
  - Nothing is deleted. Suspected duplicates are marked, not removed.
  - One date format is used for anything you create, and the original values are preserved.
  - Rows you could not interpret are left untouched and listed with a reason.
  - The change log has one line per transformation, with a date.
  - The formula column still produces the same results for rows you did not touch.
instructions:
  - Copy the sheet before you change anything, and name the copy with a date.
  - Record the row count and the sum of any numeric column before you start.
  - Standardise capitalisation and whitespace first, then look for duplicates.
  - Add a Duplicate column and mark suspected duplicates with a short note.
  - Leave ambiguous values alone and list them; do not guess.
  - Recheck the row count, the totals, and that the formula column still works.
  - Write the change log at the top of the file.
deliverable: >-
  The cleaned sheet, a marked Duplicate column, an untouched record of the original
  values, and a change log with one line per transformation.
evaluationCriteria:
  - id: nothing-deleted
    description: Every original row is still present, and suspected duplicates are marked rather than removed.
  - id: consistent-formats
    description: A single format is used for the dates and numbers you created, and it is stated in the change log.
  - id: ambiguity-flagged
    description: Values you could not interpret are left unchanged and listed with the reason.
  - id: change-log
    description: Every transformation appears in the change log, with a date, and nothing was changed that is not logged.
  - id: formulas-intact
    description: The formula column still produces the same results for the rows you did not touch.
hints:
  - Remove stray spaces and normalise capitalisation before you look for duplicates. Near-duplicates become exact ones, and exact ones are reviewable.
  - Count the rows before and after. It is the only way to know you finished.
  - If two companies look like the same company under different names, that is a question for the client, not a merge.
  - Copy the sheet rather than working in place, so a mistake is a rollback rather than a reconstruction.
commonMistakes:
  - Deleting "obvious" duplicates, which is unrecoverable when they turn out to be separate records.
  - Guessing at an ambiguous date and silently changing the meaning of a column.
  - Sorting with a filter active, which rearranges only the visible rows.
  - Tidying a column the client did not ask about, and changing a value something else depends on.
  - Cleaning the data but leaving no record of what was cleaned.
referenceGuidance: >-
  A good submission is boring: the same rows, in a consistent format, with a
  marked duplicate column and a change log somebody could reverse. If a reviewer
  cannot tell from the file alone what you changed, the cleaning is not finished.
