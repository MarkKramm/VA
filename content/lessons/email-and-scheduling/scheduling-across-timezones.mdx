A meeting that half the people miss is worse than no meeting: it costs everyone
the slot and produces no decision. Almost all of these failures are time zone
arithmetic, done in someone's head.

## Converting without guessing

Three habits remove nearly all errors:

1. **Never do the arithmetic yourself.** Use a converter, or the calendar tool's
   own time zone support. The offset between two zones is not constant — it
   changes twice a year, on different dates in different countries.
2. **Anchor to one reference point.** Give the time in the client's zone and the
   UTC time. Two reference points let anyone check the conversion.
3. **Check the day, not just the hour.** A meeting at 09:00 your time can be the
   previous day for a client twelve hours behind. "Tuesday 09:00" is ambiguous
   until you say whose Tuesday.

## An invitation that cannot be misread

A meeting invitation should answer five questions without the reader having to
convert anything:

```text
Subject:   Acme Co — March invoice review (30 min)
When:      Thu 8 Oct, 15:00–15:30 London (14:00 UTC)
Where:     Video link (below) / Phone: +44 …
Agenda:    1. March invoice discrepancy  2. April schedule
Prepare:   The March supplier statement
```

- **Duration in the subject line.** It tells the reader whether to accept.
- **The time zone named**, not implied. "15:00" with no zone is a guess.
- **An agenda**, even a two-line one. A meeting with no agenda gets cancelled or
  overruns.
- **What to bring**, if anything. Otherwise half the meeting is spent finding a
  document.

## Daylight saving, and rescheduling

Daylight saving shifts the offset between two zones by an hour, twice a year, and
not on the same date everywhere. Two consequences worth knowing:

- A **recurring** meeting that worked all winter can silently move for one side
  in spring. If a recurring meeting matters, check it after each change.
- **Avoid the hour after a transition.** Book at least a day away from the change
  if you can, and confirm with the client if you cannot.

If you have to reschedule, reschedule **early and with a reason and a proposal**:

> "I need to move Thursday's review — I'm waiting on the March statement, which
> the supplier now says arrives Friday. Same time Friday, or would Monday suit
> better?"

That is one message, and it lets the client choose. A cancellation with no
alternative is a message the client has to do work to answer.

## What not to do

Do not book a meeting without checking the other person's calendar if you have
access to it. Do not assume a client is available just because the slot is free in
your own calendar. Do not rely on "we'll work it out on the day".

## Practice

Take two cities in different countries, look up their current offsets, and write
the invitation block above for a half-hour meeting at 10:00 in your own time zone.
Then state which day it falls on for each city.
