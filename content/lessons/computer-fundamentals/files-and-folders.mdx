The single most common complaint clients have about new support staff is not
about speed or accuracy. It is that they cannot find anything. A tidy structure
is a small habit with a disproportionate effect on how professional you appear.

## Start from how you will actually look things up

There is no single correct structure, but there is a test: when someone asks for
the March invoices, can you find them in under a minute? Build backwards from
the questions you expect.

A structure that works for most client work:

```text
Clients/
  Acme Co/
    01 Inbox/          — things you have not filed yet
    02 Active/         — current projects, one folder each
    03 Reference/      — brand assets, templates, standard info
    04 Archive/        — finished or dormant, dated
```

The leading numbers are not decoration. They force the folders into a useful
order, and they keep that order stable when you add something new.

**The inbox folder is the important one.** Anything arriving from outside gets
dropped there and filed deliberately. Most "I can’t find it" problems are really
"I never filed it".

## A naming convention that actually sorts

Put the date first, in a format that sorts alphabetically into chronological
order, and describe the content rather than its state:

```text
2026-03-14_AcmeCo_Invoice_March.docx
2026-03-14_AcmeCo_Invoice_March_final.docx   ← avoid
```

`YYYY-MM-DD` first sorts correctly every time, in every file manager, forever.
Whatever date format your client uses internally, use this one for files you
create.

Describe what the thing **is**, not what stage it is at. "March invoice" is a
fact. "final_v2_REALLY_final" is a record of your anxiety, and it breaks the
moment two people edit the same file.

## What not to do

Do not use a single folder called "Desktop". Do not mix client work and personal
files. Do not rely on colour-coding as the only signal — it disappears when
someone else opens the file, and it is invisible to anyone with a different kind
of colour vision.

## The test that matters

Before you consider a structure finished, close the folder and try to find
something you know exists but have not looked at in a while. If you cannot, the
structure is wrong, no matter how tidy it looks.
