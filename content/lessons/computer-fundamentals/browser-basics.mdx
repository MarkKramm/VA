A browser is the tool you will use more than any other. Almost every VA task
happens in one. The features below are not advanced, but using them
deliberately saves hours a month.

## Tabs are a to-do list, not a graveyard

The default habit — opening a tab for every link and leaving it — produces
forty tabs and no idea which is which. Two habits fix it:

**Name tabs when you open them.** Middle-click (or ctrl-click) to open a link in
a background tab rather than replacing what you are reading, and rename the ones
that matter. A tab called "Acme invoice" is worth more than any tab-management
extension.

**Close as you go, on purpose.** At the end of any task, close what you opened.
This is unglamorous and it is the difference between a browser you can think in
and one you have to search.

## Bookmarks are a system, not a graveyard

A bookmark bar with 400 untitled links is worse than no bookmark bar, because
you now believe you have saved something.

A structure that scales:

```text
★ Work
    Clients
        Acme Co
    Tools
    Reference
★ Learn
★ Read later
```

Put anything you will need again into a named folder within a week, or do not
bookmark it. A bookmark you will not find again is just a file you have
forgotten about.

## Keep client work separate from personal browsing

This is the most important habit on this page, and it is a security habit as much
as an organisation one.

Use a **separate browser profile** — or a separate browser — for client work.
Then:

- Your client’s accounts are not sitting in a profile that also has your personal
  email, your bank, and your social media logged in.
- A malicious site you encounter during research cannot reach anything personal.
- If a client relationship ends, you can delete or export that profile cleanly and
  be certain nothing of theirs is left behind.

Some clients will ask you to use a specific password manager or a specific
browser. Follow their instruction — but the separate-profile habit survives
almost every variation of it, and the alternative is one leaked password away
from a much larger problem.

## Downloads deserve a habit too

Set your download location to a single folder rather than accepting the default
every time. Then file from that folder, using the naming convention from the
files and folders lesson.

Two specific things to watch for:

**Check what you are opening.** A file named `Invoice_March.pdf` is the second most
common way malware arrives in this work. If a client sends an unexpected
attachment, ask before opening it. It costs one message.

**Be careful with links in email.** Hover before you click, and prefer navigating
to a known site directly over following a link. The cybersecurity module covers
this in full — it is one of the highest-value things on this platform, because
VA work attracts job and client scams deliberately.
