A client's instructions are usually one paragraph containing six tasks, two of
which are actually the same task and one of which is missing a detail you need.
Turning that into a list is most of the skill of administrative support.

## Splitting instructions into tasks

Read the instructions once without writing anything. Then read again and split on
**verbs** — every action the client wants is a task.

> _"Can you tidy up the shared folder, chase the invoice from the printing
> company, and also I think we should move the newsletter to the new template —
> and let me know if we're running low on anything."_

That is four tasks, not one:

1. Tidy the shared folder
2. Chase the printing company invoice
3. Move the newsletter to the new template
4. Check stock levels

Two of them ("tidy", "move") are big enough to need their own sub-steps. The other
two are single actions.

## Finding the gaps before you start

For each task, check that you know the **who, what, where and when**. Anything you
cannot answer is a question, and questions go to the client before you start, not
halfway through.

From the example above:

| Task                   | Missing                                            |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Tidy the shared folder | Which folder, and what counts as tidy?             |
| Chase the invoice      | Which invoice, and how firm should the message be? |
| Move the newsletter    | Which template, and does the old one get deleted?  |
| Check stock levels     | Where is the stock recorded?                       |

Sending one message with four questions is far better than sending four messages
over two days. It respects the client's time and it means you are not blocked
later.

## A format the client can correct

Send the list back. It takes you two minutes and saves an hour of wrong work:

```text
From your message, I'm going to do these four things:
1. Tidy the shared folder — I'll use the same structure as the client drive.
2. Chase the printing invoice — polite first reminder, cc you.
3. Move the newsletter to the new template.
4. Check stock levels against the inventory sheet.

Two things I need before I start:
- Which folder counts as "the shared folder" — the team one or the client one?
- Should the old newsletter template be deleted once moved?
```

The client can now reply "yes, team one, keep the old template" in one line. That
is the whole point.

## What not to do

Do not start work on an ambiguous task and hope. Do not ask questions one at a time
across a day. Do not silently drop the instruction you did not understand — the
client will assume it was done.

## Practice

Write a paragraph of instructions for a task in your own life, then turn it into a
task list with the gaps marked. You will usually find at least one instruction you
could not have followed without asking.
