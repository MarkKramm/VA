A client reads your message between two other things. Write it so they can
understand it in one pass and reply without opening anything else.

## The first line carries the message

Put the point in the first sentence. The greeting is one word; the first sentence
is the message.

**Weak:**

> Hi! Hope you're well. I've been working through the list you sent over on
> Tuesday and I've got most of it done, but I've hit something with the third item
> that I wanted to check with you about before I go further…

**Strong:**

> Hi — I've finished items 1, 2 and 4. Item 3 needs a decision from you before I
> continue.

The second version took two lines and the client knows exactly what to do. The
first version makes them read to the end to find out whether anything is wrong.

## Asking so it is easy to answer

Every question should be answerable with one word, one number, or one choice. If
it needs an essay, it is not a question — it is a conversation, and it should be a
short call or a shared document instead.

- **Not:** "What do you think we should do about the client portal?"
- **Yes:** "Should I use the existing portal or set up a new folder? Existing /
  new — either works, I just need to know which."

Give the options. It is faster for them and it shows you have thought about it.

## Reporting a problem

Three sentences, in this order:

1. **What happened** — the fact, no drama.
2. **What it affects** — the consequence, honestly.
3. **What you propose** — your suggested next step.

> The supplier hasn't sent the invoice I was expecting, so the reconciliation for
> March is missing one line. I can send the rest today and add the line when it
> arrives, or hold the whole thing until Friday. Which do you prefer?

No apology for things that are not your fault, and no hiding them either. A client
who finds out late is a client who stops trusting your updates. "I should have
checked earlier" is worth saying once, when it is true, and never as a reflex.

## What not to do

Do not write a paragraph when a line will do. Do not use "just" to soften every
sentence — "just checking in" makes your work sound smaller than it is. Do not
send a message with no clear next action for either of you.

## Practice

Take the last message you sent about work and rewrite it with the point in the
first line. Then count how many sentences it took to say something the reader
could act on.
