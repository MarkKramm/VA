Almost nobody makes data entry mistakes because they cannot read. They make them
because of a small number of predictable situations, and each one has a habit that
prevents it.

## Where errors actually come from

- **Transposition** — swapping two characters. `4182` becomes `4128`. The most
  common error, and the hardest to spot, because both look plausible.
- **Wrong row or column** — entering a value one cell off. Happens when the
  source and the destination are laid out differently.
- **Unit and format drift** — `01/02/2026` is January in one country and February
  in another. `1,500` is fifteen hundred in one locale and one and a half in
  another.
- **Interruption** — the real cause of most of the above. A phone call mid-row is
  how a value lands in the wrong place.
- **Copying a formula down too far**, which silently writes a value where none
  was meant to exist.

## Verifying your own work

Reading back what you typed does not work: you read what you meant, not what is
there. Three methods that do work:

1. **Enter in one direction, verify in another.** If you typed down a column,
   check across the row. The change of direction forces your eye to process the
   data rather than the pattern.
2. **Check totals and counts.** Before you start, count the source rows. After you
   finish, count the destination rows. They should match, and if the data has a
   column that should sum, sum it in both places. Two independent checks, and the
   arithmetic does the work.
3. **Spot-check the extremes.** The first row, the last row, and the largest and
   smallest values. Errors cluster at boundaries — the end of a session, the
   longest value, the row you did last.

Say the number out loud while entering it if you are working alone. It sounds
ridiculous and it halves transposition errors, because you are using a second
channel to check the first.

## Keeping a record

A simple log turns "I think I entered about two hundred rows" into an answer:

```text
Source:      acme-contacts-2026-09.xlsx  (241 rows)
Entered to:  CRM import sheet, 2026-10-05
Rows:        241 of 241
Checks:      row count matched; email column spot-checked first/last 5
Skipped:     3 rows with no email — left blank, listed in the notes column
```

The last line is the one that matters. Anything you skipped, guessed or could not
read is a question for the client, and it is far easier to ask at the time than to
explain later.

## What not to do

Do not fix an error silently in a source file that belongs to the client — record
it and tell them. Do not "tidy" data while entering it: if a phone number is
formatted differently, enter it as it is and raise it separately. Do not enter
data in a hurry without a row count, because then you have no way to know you
finished.

## Practice

Enter twenty rows of contact data from any source into a blank sheet, then verify
with a different method: count the rows, check the first and last, and spot-check
the longest value in each column. Write the four-line log above for what you did.
