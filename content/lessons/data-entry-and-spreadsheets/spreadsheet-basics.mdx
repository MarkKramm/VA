A client's spreadsheet is a small program. It has structure, hidden columns,
formulas and often a person who maintains it. Your job is to add to it, not to
reinterpret it.

## Reading a sheet before you touch it

Before you type anything:

1. **Read the header row.** It is the contract for every column. `Amount` and
   `Amount (net)` are different columns.
2. **Click a cell that looks computed.** The formula bar tells you whether the
   value is typed or calculated. A column that looks like data but is a formula is
   the single most common source of accidental damage.
3. **Look for hidden rows, hidden columns and other sheets.** Filtered views hide
   rows without deleting them, and a second sheet often holds the lookup lists.
4. **Look for a frozen header or a filter.** A filter means somebody is using this
   sheet to see a subset of it. Sorting while a filter is active rearranges only
   the visible rows — a classic and invisible way to scramble a dataset.

## The formulas worth knowing

Five functions cover most administrative spreadsheet work. Learn these before
anything else:

| Formula                                | What it does               | Typical use                       |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| `SUM(range)`                           | Adds numbers               | Totals, invoice values            |
| `COUNTA(range)`                        | Counts non-empty cells     | "How many rows did I enter?"      |
| `IF(test, a, b)`                       | Chooses between two values | Flagging overdue items            |
| `VLOOKUP(value, table, column, FALSE)` | Finds a matching row       | Matching a name to an ID          |
| `TRIM(text)`                           | Removes stray spaces       | Fixing values that will not match |

Two rules make them safe:

- **Absolute references** — a dollar sign before the column letter and the row
  number — when a range must not shift as you copy the formula down. Forgetting
  them is why the second row's formula looks up the wrong table.
- **`TRIM` before you match.** A trailing space in a name means `VLOOKUP` finds
  nothing, and the two values look identical on screen.

## Editing safely

- **Never sort a filtered range** without clearing the filter first.
- **Insert rows rather than pasting over**, so formulas below the data extend
  correctly.
- **Do not type over a formula cell.** If you must replace a calculated value with
  a typed one, say so in your handover — somebody is relying on it recalculating.
- **Copy the sheet before a big change.** `Sheet name (backup 2026-10-05)` is
  cheap insurance, and deleting it afterwards is a decision you can make once you
  know the change was right.
- **Check the total before and after.** If you added rows, the sum should change
  by the amount you added. If it did not, you have entered data outside the range
  the formula covers.

## What not to do

Do not restructure a client's sheet because a different layout would be tidier.
Do not delete columns that look empty — they may feed a formula or a chart. Do not
leave a filter active when you finish, because the next person will think rows are
missing.

## Practice

Take any small spreadsheet, find one calculated column, and write down what the
formula does in one sentence. Then add a row at the bottom and check whether the
total changed — if it did not, you have found a range that does not extend.
