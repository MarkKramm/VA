A brief is not a diary of your research. It is the shortest document that lets the
client act without doing any of the work again.

## Put the answer first

Start with the answer, in one or two sentences. Then the detail. Then the sources.
This is the opposite of how the work felt to do — you spent an hour finding out —
but the client's first question is "what did you find", not "how did you get
there".

A structure that works for almost every brief:

```text
Answer        — one or two sentences, the actual finding
Detail        — the three to five things that matter, with numbers if relevant
Sources       — what each claim rests on, with links
Uncertainty   — what you could not confirm, and what you would check next
```

If the answer is "it depends", say what it depends on. "It depends on whether the
company is VAT registered" is a useful answer. "It's complicated" is not.

## Showing your sources

Attach the source to the claim, not to the end of the document. A reader should be
able to check any single sentence without hunting.

A pattern that reads well:

> **Answer:** Invoices must be kept for six years from the end of the accounting
> period. ([HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/…))

One link per claim is usually enough. If a claim rests on two sources that agree,
say so once. If two sources disagree, show both and label the disagreement — do
not silently choose.

## Length and structure

Short is a feature, not a shortcut. Aim for the length where you have said
everything true and nothing else:

- Headings, so it can be skimmed.
- Bullets for lists of facts, prose for reasoning.
- No throat-clearing. "I researched this and found the following" adds nothing.
- No adjectives doing the work of evidence. "Very reliable" is not a finding;
  "the page has not been updated since 2021" is.

## What not to do

Do not paste long extracts. Do not include every source you opened — include the
ones the claim rests on. Do not bury a problem in the middle; if you could not
confirm something, it goes in the brief, not in a footnote.

## Practice

Write a brief of no more than 200 words answering a real question — where a
company is registered, what a service costs, when a deadline falls. Use the four
headings above and attach one source per claim.
