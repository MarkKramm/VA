A queue of twenty written requests is not twenty tasks. It is four groups, and
sorting them first is what stops the session becoming twenty half-finished things.

## Sorting the queue

Read everything once, without doing anything. Then sort into four piles:

1. **Do now** — you have everything you need and it takes minutes. Clear these
   first: they shrink the queue and they are usually the ones somebody is waiting
   on.
2. **Ask** — you cannot start because a detail is missing. Draft the question
   immediately, even if you send the batch later. Asking early means the answer
   arrives while you work on something else.
3. **Work** — you have everything you need and it takes real time. These get
   scheduled into blocks.
4. **Defer** — not urgent, not blocking anyone, and not today. Put it in the list
   with a date. This is the pile that must be written down, because it is the one
   you will forget.

The single most common failure in queue work is doing the easy items repeatedly
and never starting the hard ones. If the "work" pile has not been touched by the
end of the session, the session did not accomplish anything the client asked for.

## Processing without losing threads

- **One item at a time, to completion.** Opening five and finishing none leaves
  five things to re-read.
- **Write the state down the moment you leave an item.** "Waiting on Acme's
  address" — not "in progress". A queue item with no next action is a queue item
  you will open twice.
- **Keep a scratch list** of anything you touched but did not finish. At the end of
  the session it becomes the handover; during it, it stops you reopening things.
- **Batch the questions.** Four questions in one message get answered in one
  reply. Four messages across a day get answered across a day, and one gets
  forgotten.

## Closing the session

End every session with a status the client can read in five seconds. Three lines is
usually enough:

```text
Done:      March reconciliation, 4 draft replies, folder tidy-up
Waiting:   2 questions sent (supplier statement, April dates)
Next:      April schedule — tomorrow morning
```

This is not reporting for its own sake. It is the thing that lets a client trust a
written-only relationship, because it is the only evidence they have that the
queue moved.

## What not to do

Do not answer a request you do not understand just to clear it. Do not leave an
item "in progress" with no next action. Do not silently drop a request you decided
was unimportant — say "I've left this until Thursday, is that right?" and let the
client disagree.

## Practice

Take ten written requests — real or invented — and sort them into the four piles
without doing any of them. Then write the three-line closing status for the
session you would run.
