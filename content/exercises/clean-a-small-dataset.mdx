#### Build the mess

Create a sheet of about twenty companies. Then make it messy on purpose:

- Write two of them twice, once with a trailing space and once without.
- Use three capitalisations: `ACME CO`, `Acme Co`, `acme co`.
- Enter dates in two formats: `01/02/2026` and `2026-02-01`.
- Put a comma thousands separator in some amounts and not others.
- Leave one row with a company name but no other data.
- Add one row that could be the same company under a different name.

#### What to produce

```text
Sheet 1  raw            — untouched
Sheet 2  cleaned        — your work
Column H Duplicate?     — "yes" + a short note, or blank
Change log              — one line per transformation, with a date
```

#### How it will be judged

- **Nothing deleted.** The whole exercise is about the difference between marking
  and removing.
- **Standardise before you look for duplicates.** Near-duplicates become exact
  ones once the spaces and capitalisation are consistent, which is when a
  duplicate check is worth anything.
- **Ambiguous dates are left alone.** `01/02/2026` is January in one country and
  February in another. Guessing changes the meaning of the data; flagging does
  not.
- **The change log is complete.** If somebody reverted your work they should be
  able to see exactly what they reverted.
- **The near-match under a different name is flagged, not merged.** That is the
  judgement call, and the right answer is a question to the client.
